# 07 — Build a research dossier that survives revision

Choose a theorem or reduction in your own research area; birational geometry is the intended research track. Analysis and linear algebra provide accessible alternatives. Supply three relevant accessible sources, precise locators, and a known control case. If only one source is available, record the limitation rather than inventing a literature survey.

Propose one precise improvement: weaker assumptions, an extension to a different geometric setting, a new reduction, or a more explicit construction. Compare two proof routes. Attempt the hardest shared bridge before recommending the project. A demonstrated obstruction can be the correct result.

Integrate target records, retrieval/applicability checks, attempted proofs, independent checking where available, memory revisions, and an admission decision. Formalize a tractable lemma if a working environment exists; keep the unformalized bridge visible.

Adversarial review: change one hypothesis; submit a delayed report for the old target; withdraw a shared lemma; supply a correct proof of a weaker statement. Restart from saved files in a fresh session and recover the supported conclusions.

Deliver a reproducible dossier: source extracts/locators within permitted use, versioned statements, original attempts, exact calculations, failed routes, formal evidence if actually obtained, and unresolved obligations. End with pursue/revise/stop and its mathematical reason.

Assessment: specificity of improvement; correctness of the decisive argument; source applicability; resistance to the four faults; successful restart. File count and number of agents earn no credit. A detailed plan with no attempted bridge is incomplete. A rigorous negative result with a revised target can be excellent.


## Birational geometry route

Start from a proof passage in a paper you actually use. For a proposed extension, isolate the first invocation whose hypotheses no longer hold. Record the ambient category, dimension, singularity assumptions, morphism hypotheses, and positivity conditions only where the actual theorem requires them. Do not fill in a generic checklist in place of reading the result.

Distinguish: existence of a model or operation; preservation of the required properties; termination of an iteration; and the implication returning to the original target. Attempt a replacement for one missing step. If your theorem package is mutually dependent, draw arrows for actual logical uses and supply a well-founded rank or an independent proof that breaks a cycle.

A change from projective to compact Kähler geometry is a possible investigation, not an automatic transfer principle. Verify each cited result in the new category. An obstruction to one method does not disprove the desired theorem.

The central deliverable is proof progress, not numerical output. Elementary calculations elsewhere in this pack are calibration exercises and can be abbreviated once their system-design lessons are understood.
