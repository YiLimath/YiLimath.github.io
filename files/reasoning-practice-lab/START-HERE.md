# Research systems: adversarial practice pack

Use a chatbox or Codex. No programming prerequisite. Work in a separate folder; keep existing research files untouched. Ask the assistant to read one mission and create its deliverables. Do not give it the reference answers until after the first attempt.

Each mission has three rounds: solve, diagnose a deliberate fault, and transfer to a changed target. Preserve the first response verbatim. A polished revised response is not a substitute for the record of what went wrong. Reference answers are mathematical controls, not results from measured agent runs.

Suggested sequence: 01 target precision; 02 evidence; 03 method selection; 04 quantifiers; 05 transformations; 06 formal admission; 07 capstone. Stop at the first unresolved inference and work on it. Difficulty comes from producing and defending evidence, not from filling forms.

Starter instruction:
> Read RESEARCH.md and mission 01. State the exact target before solving it. Produce a witness, counterexample, or explicitly conditional argument. Save your first attempt separately from later review. Do not claim tools ran unless you have their actual output. Do not read reference/answers.md yet.

To assess your system, compare an ordinary single conversation with your structured workflow on the same tasks. Keep task inputs and available evidence comparable. Record failures, repairs, unnecessary work, and unresolved cases. These are small educational experiments, not statistical performance claims.

For a fresh-session test, supply only the saved records and ask which claims can be reused. For a harder test, introduce a target revision and one delayed report. The challenge is to retain valid work without importing an obsolete verdict.

## Proof-based research route

If your subject is birational geometry, use missions 01–02 briefly to calibrate target and evidence handling, then focus on 05–07 and the course hands-on page's research track. Missions 03–04 are optional accessible demonstrations. Hands-on means attempting proofs, auditing hypotheses, constructing counterexamples, and repairing reductions; it does not require numerical experimentation. Bring an actual paper and a precise proof passage to the capstone.
